Executive overview

Three statutes, one safety net

NIIRA 2025 rewrites the rules of the insurance market. The IRC Bill 2025 rebuilds the regulator. The NDIC Act 2023 governs the deposit insurer that sits beside, not inside, the insurance regime. Click any tile, card or node for narrative and insight.

Minimum capital ladder (NIIRA s.15)

Each figure is a floor. The binding requirement is the higher of the naira floor and the risk-based capital the Commission determines (s.15(1)). Existing insurers had 12 months from 31 July 2025 to comply (s.15(6)).

Act No. 14 of 2025 · Assented 31 July 2025 · Gazetted 29 August 2025

NIIRA 2025 Explorer

232 sections across 14 Parts plus miscellaneous provisions and schedules. It repeals the Insurance Act 2004, the Marine Insurance Act, the Motor Vehicles (Third Party Insurance) Act, the NICON Act and the Nigeria Reinsurance Corporation Act. Open any Part for the provisions, the practical reading and the penalties.

Repeals the National Insurance Commission Act 1997

Insurance Regulatory Commission Bill 2025

NAICOM becomes the Insurance Regulatory Commission (IRC), an independent body with sharper supervisory, intervention and enforcement tools. The Senate passed the Bill at third reading in July 2026 following the Committee report presented by Senator Adetokunbo Abiru. Open the cards for provisions and implications.

Funds of the Commission (s.16): how every naira of resources is split
Operating Fund 50%
Education Fund 30%
Security & Insurance Development Fund 20%
General Reserve: net operating surplus

Resources include a 1% levy on every insurance institution (s.15(1)(b)): on gross premium income for insurers and reinsurers, on gross contribution or income for Takaful, insurtech and web aggregators, on gross commission for brokers and on gross fees for loss adjusters. Payable on or before 30 September each year.

NDIC Act 2023: the insurance-relevant provisions

Deposit insurance is insurance in economic substance but sits outside NIIRA by design. NIIRA s.2(2)(c) excludes deposit insurance carried out by the NDIC, and NDIC Act s.87 exempts the Corporation from the Insurance Act in its role as deposit insurer. Only the parts that touch insurance businesses, policyholders and the wider safety net are covered here.

Maximum insured deposit per depositor (s.25, as varied by the Board in 2024)

Statutory floors in s.25(1): N500,000 (non-MFB) and N200,000 (MFB). Section 25(2) lets the Board vary the maximum upwards; in May 2024 the coverage became N5m (DMBs), N2m (MFBs, PMBs, PSBs) and N5m per subscriber of mobile money operators. All accounts held in the same right and capacity in one failed institution are merged as one (s.25(3)).

Failed insurer waterfall (s.110(5))

The Protection Fund loan is modelled as a first-line charge ranking immediately after liquidation expenses; the statute (s.212(6)) does not state its rank against those expenses.

Policyholder recovery with the Protection Fund (s.212)

The Insurance Policyholders Protection Fund pays admitted claims that an insolvent insurer cannot, then recovers as a first-line charge from the liquidation. Compare recovery with and without the Fund.

Professional caveat

This Navigator is an educational and analytical tool published by FinPolNomics GREEN FINANCE and FinPolNomics ANALYTICA. It summarises and models the Nigerian Insurance Industry Reform Act 2025, the Insurance Regulatory Commission Bill 2025 as passed by the National Assembly, and the insurance-relevant provisions of the Nigeria Deposit Insurance Corporation Act 2023. It is not legal, tax, actuarial, investment or regulatory advice and does not create any professional relationship.

Statutory text prevails over every summary here. Calculators use illustrative parameters where the law leaves a figure to the Commission, the Corporation or the market (for example risk-based capital charges, premium rates, bank rates and deposit insurance premium rates). Verify figures against the Gazette, current circulars, guidelines and professional advice before acting. Bill provisions may change before and after assent. Monetary values are in Nigerian naira unless stated. The author and publishers accept no liability for decisions taken in reliance on this material.
